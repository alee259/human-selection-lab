SIMULATION SETTINGS

Evolutionary Forces

Choose which evolutionary forces affect the population.

Population
? Number of diploid individuals in the population. Smaller populations experience stronger genetic drift.
? Initial proportion of allele A in the population. For example, 0.2 means 20% A and 80% a. A is not necessarily the dominant allele.
? Number of generations simulated. More generations allow evolutionary forces more opportunities to change allele frequencies.
Natural Selection
? Relative reproductive success of individuals with two copies of allele A.
? Relative reproductive success of heterozygous individuals carrying one A and one a allele.
? Relative reproductive success of individuals with two copies of allele a.
Mutation
? Probability that an a allele mutates into A during one generation.
? Probability that an A allele mutates into a during one generation.
Gene Flow
? Fraction of the receiving population's gene pool contributed by migrants each generation. For example, 0.1 represents 10%.
? Frequency of allele A in the population supplying migrants. Migration can raise or lower the receiving population's frequency.
? Number of initial generations during which migration occurs. For example, 10 means migration acts in generations 1 through 10.

Each generation applies enabled forces in this order: Mutation → Gene Flow → Selection → Drift. This order is a modeling assumption and can affect the results.
